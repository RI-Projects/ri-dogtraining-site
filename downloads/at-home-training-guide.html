At-Home Training Guide

The Ultimate Guide to a Calm Family Dog

Welcome to Your At-Home Training Journey

Training isn't just about teaching tricks; it's about establishing clear communication, boundaries, and a healthy relationship with your dog. This guide will walk you through the essential steps to cultivate a calm, balanced family dog right in your living room.

1. Establish the "Place" Command

The "Place" command is your dog's off-switch. By teaching your dog to go to a designated bed or cot and stay there until released, you can prevent door-dashing, begging at the table, and over-excitement when guests arrive.

  • Start Small: Lure your dog onto the bed, reward them, and release them immediately.
  • Build Duration: Slowly increase the time they spend on "Place" before rewarding.
  • Add Distractions: Practice while you cook dinner or watch TV.

2. Prevent Resource Guarding

Resource guarding occurs when a dog feels anxious about losing access to high-value items (like food, toys, or even the couch). The key is to teach your dog that human presence means good things, not that things are being taken away.

  • Trade Up: If your dog has something they shouldn't, never just snatch it away. Offer a higher-value treat in exchange.
  • Hand Feeding: Hand-feed meals occasionally so they associate your hands near their food with positive experiences.

3. Structured Play & Enrichment

A tired dog is a good dog, but mental fatigue is just as important as physical exercise. Integrate structured play and mental enrichment into their daily routine.

  • Tug-of-War with Rules: Teach your dog "Drop It" during tug sessions. The game only continues when they follow the rules.
  • Puzzle Toys: Feed meals out of Kongs or snuffle mats to make them work for their food.

4. The Importance of Crate Training

A crate is not a punishment; it is your dog's safe den. When used properly, it prevents destructive behavior and helps your dog learn to settle down.

  • Make the crate a positive place by feeding meals inside.
  • Never use the crate out of anger.
  • Ensure they have ample exercise before long crating periods.
