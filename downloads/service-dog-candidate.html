IS MY DOG A SERVICE DOG CANDIDATE?

Understanding the Extreme Standards of Public Access
Service dogs perform life-changing work for their handlers, but the reality is that the vast majority of dogs do not have the genetics, temperament, or nerve strength required to be a legitimate service dog.

Emotional Support vs. Service Dog

An Emotional Support Animal (ESA) provides comfort simply by being present, but they have no legal public access rights outside of housing. A Service Dog is legally defined by the ADA as a dog specifically trained to perform a task directly related to a person's disability, and they are granted public access.

The Traits of a True Candidate

1. Bulletproof Nerves
A service dog cannot be easily startled. They must be able to walk through a busy grocery store with shopping carts crashing, children screaming, and automatic doors opening without flinching or breaking focus from their handler.
2. Zero Reactivity
A dog with any history of human or dog aggression, extreme fearfulness, or high prey drive (chasing squirrels/cats) is immediately disqualified from service work. A service dog must be completely neutral to the environment.

The Standard

Training a service dog often takes 1.5 to 2 years of daily, rigorous exposure and obedience work. If your dog washes out (fails to meet the standard), it is not a failure—it simply means they are meant to be a great pet rather than a working dog.
