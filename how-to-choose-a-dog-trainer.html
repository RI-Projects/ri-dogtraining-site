How to Choose a Dog Trainer

Not all dog training is equal. Here are seven things worth checking before you hand over your dog — and your money — anywhere in the Phoenix Valley.

See Programs & Pricing Book a Call

Picking a dog trainer is a real decision — you're trusting someone with your dog and your money. Before you commit anywhere in the Phoenix Valley, run through this checklist.

1. Ask how they actually train

A good trainer can explain their approach in plain language and tell you why, not just what. We train on consistency, leadership, and understanding — building dogs that think and adapt, not ones that only obey when a treat is visible.

2. Make sure the owner is trained too

If a program hands your dog back with no owner training, the results won't last. The handler has to learn the tools. Every one of our programs includes hands-on owner training for exactly this reason.

3. Look for transparency

Clear pricing, clear timelines, and honest progress updates. We publish our prices and send video updates throughout Board & Train — you should never be guessing what you're paying for or how your dog is doing.

4. Ask about your specific dog

Reactive, anxious, a stubborn adult, a blank-slate puppy — a good trainer tailors the plan instead of running every dog through the same script.

5. Check experience and consistency

How long have they trained? Do they stand behind their work with follow-up support? We offer 30-day follow-up on our advanced programs because the first weeks home matter most.

6. Prioritize local

A local trainer knows the environments your dog actually lives in — Valley heat, busy sidewalks, dog parks, open acreage. We're based in Chandler and serve Gilbert, Tempe, Mesa, Queen Creek, and the greater Phoenix Valley.

7. Trust the conversation

The first call should feel like a real conversation about your dog and your goals, not a hard sell. If it doesn't, keep looking.
